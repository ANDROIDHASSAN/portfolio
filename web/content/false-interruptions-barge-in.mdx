There are two ways to build an annoying voice agent.

The first one never lets you interrupt. You realise in the first five words that it misunderstood you, and you have to sit through the remaining thirty like a hostage.

The second one stops talking every time you breathe. You cough, it stops. You say "hmm" to show you're listening, it stops and asks what you said. You never hear a full sentence.

Good barge-in lives between the two, and getting there takes more than one setting.

## What barge-in actually is

When the caller starts speaking while the agent is talking, the agent should stop, quickly, and listen. That's barge-in. The basic version is simple: a voice activity detector (VAD) watches the caller's audio, and when it detects speech during agent playback, playback stops.

The problem is that a VAD detects **sound that looks like speech**, not **intent to take the turn**. And plenty of sounds look like speech:

- coughs, laughs, a sharp breath
- a TV, a colleague, traffic
- the agent's *own voice* leaking back through the caller's speaker
- **backchannels**: "hmm", "okay", "right", "haan", "achha"

That last group is the interesting one.

## Backchannels are not interruptions

In conversation, listeners constantly make small noises that mean "go on, I'm with you". Linguists call them backchannels. They are the opposite of an interruption: they're the listener *declining* the turn.

Indian callers backchannel a lot, and in two or three languages. "Haan haan", "achha", "ji", "okay okay". An agent that treats each of those as barge-in will stop mid-sentence over and over, and the caller will hear an agent that can't finish a thought.

## Fix 1: recover from false interruptions

The most useful idea here is to not treat an interruption as final until it proves itself. If the caller makes a sound, pause. If that sound turns into real words, the caller has the turn. If it doesn't, **resume the sentence** where it stopped.

In LiveKit Agents that's a couple of options on the session ([this site's agent](https://github.com/ANDROIDHASSAN/portfolio/blob/main/voice-agent/agent.py)):

```python
turn_handling=TurnHandlingOptions(
    interruption={"resume_false_interruption": True, "false_interruption_timeout": 1.0},
    ...
)
```

If no transcript arrives within a second of the "interruption", the agent decides it was a cough and carries on. One second is long enough for real speech to show up in the transcript and short enough that a genuine pause doesn't feel like the agent froze.

## Fix 2: require some evidence before stopping

Stopping instantly on any sound feels responsive in demos and broken on real calls. Frameworks let you require a minimum duration of speech, or a minimum number of transcribed words, before an interruption counts. Tune it per audience:

| Setting | Too low | Too high |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum speech before stopping | Coughs and "hmm" stop the agent | Callers feel ignored when they cut in |
| False-interruption timeout | Agent resumes over a caller who was just slow to start | Long dead air after every cough |

There's no universal right answer. A screening call where people answer questions needs different settings from a support line where people are angry and want to interrupt.

## Fix 3: remove the noise before the VAD sees it

Every false interruption you prevent upstream is one you don't have to recover from. Two layers:

- **In the browser**: echo cancellation, noise suppression and auto gain on the mic. Echo cancellation matters most, because otherwise the agent hears its own voice through the caller's laptop speakers and interrupts itself.
- **In the agent**: a dedicated noise-cancellation model on the incoming audio. This agent runs Krisp's BVC (background voice cancellation), which removes background speakers as well as noise, so a colleague talking nearby doesn't trigger barge-in.

```python
room_options=room_io.RoomOptions(
    audio_input=room_io.AudioInputOptions(noise_cancellation=noise_cancellation.BVC())
)
```

## Fix 4: know when the caller is *done*

Interruption has a twin problem: end-of-turn detection. If the agent decides the caller has finished while they're just pausing to think, it starts talking over them, and now the *caller* is the one interrupting. Same bad experience, other direction.

Silence-based detection ("they stopped for 500 ms, so they're done") fails on thoughtful speakers and on code-mixed speech, where people often pause while switching languages. Semantic turn detection, a model that looks at *what* was said ("did that sentence end?"), fails much less. It's also one of the biggest slices of a latency budget, which is why I treat it as the main target in [the sub-second Hindi agent plan](/blog/sub-second-hindi-voice-agent).

## The combined picture

A good interruption stack is layered:

1. Clean the audio (echo cancellation, noise and background-voice cancellation).
2. Require a little evidence before stopping.
3. If it turns out to be nothing, resume the sentence.
4. Detect the end of the caller's turn semantically, not just by silence.

None of these is clever on its own. Together they're the difference between an agent that sounds like it's listening and one that sounds like it's flinching.
